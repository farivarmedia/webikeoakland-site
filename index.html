Show Me The Separation

A network of separated bikeways is emerging Downtown and around Lake Merritt via a bevy of recently completed, underway, and planned projects.

14th St, Franklin St, Lakeshore Ave, and the Oakland–Alameda Access Project are under construction. 27th St & Bay Place is up next. The project on Lakeside Dr & Lake Merritt Blvd is moving forward. Construction is expected soon on Martin Luther King Jr Way (2nd St to 14th St). The East Bay Greenway will soon start on 10th St/E 10th St and lead to Fruitvale and, eventually, all the way to San Leandro and beyond. Oakland is working on final designs for Grand Ave, and developing and seeking funding for even more.

7th/E 8th St was built via a routine paving job in 2025, and projects on 20th St (Broadway to Harrison) and around Grand Ave and Harrison St (see the Lakeside Family Streets story) are wrapping up.

Completed — June 2026

Lakeside Family Streets

In June 2026, OakDOT completed the Lakeside Family Streets project, which added one-half mile of separated bike lanes along Lake Merritt. The project included Harrison St from Thomas L. Berkley Way to 27th St, and Grand Ave from Harrison St to Bay Pl. It also built a protected intersection at Harrison St/Grand Ave and repurposed the slip turn at this location to be part of the two-way separated bike lanes along the lake.

The project built upon the Lakeside Green Streets project, completed in 2019, which made major improvements to the downtown edge of Lake Merritt. Lakeside Family Streets is key to OakDOT's development of separated bike lanes, connecting to recently completed work on 20th St and Lakeside Dr and to upcoming work on 27th St and Grand Ave.

New data

Oakland Bikes to BART

BART recently published a Station Profile Study based on 2024 data — its largest customer survey since 2015. Among the findings: region-wide data on people biking from home to BART, and Oakland stations top the list. Fully 37% of all bicycle trips from home to BART are to stations in Oakland.

Out of BART's 48 non-airport stations, the top five busiest for these bike trips are all in Oakland: Fruitvale, MacArthur, West Oakland, Lake Merritt, and 19th Street. By mode share, Lake Merritt BART leaves the competition behind, with 17% of BART customers traveling from home to this station by bicycle. (MacArthur came in second at 14%.) See BART's data at bart.gov/about/reports/profile.

20th Street Makeover

Sometimes mysteries lie below the surface, and that's certainly true of Oakland's streets. In 2025, construction of the Thomas L. Berkley Way (20th St) project faced major delays due to the discovery of unknown utility lines. Now, the final stretch offers separated bicycle lanes leading right to the 19th Street BART entrance. Delivered through an Active Transportation Program grant, this project also includes new trees, widened sidewalks, and shorter crosswalks.

Completed

A Path Through the Redwoods

Repurposing the "slip turn" at Harrison St/Grand Ave for two-way bike lanes is great for cyclists traveling around Lake Merritt. But for bicyclists continuing north on Harrison St, how do you get there?

The protagonists in this tale include a "pork chop" island, two redwood trees, advocates, and problem-solving City staff. Rather than cutting into the pork chop island and putting the redwoods at risk, a path was built up over the island to protect the trees' roots. And that's how Oakland got a path through the redwoods in the heart of the Town.

In progress

18th Street Remade

18th Street is a wide road in West Oakland that splits into the one-way couplet of 17th and 18th Streets near Market St, then heads across I-980 and into downtown. The portion from Market St to Martin Luther King Jr Way was paved in late spring 2026 and restriped with buffered bike lanes, mainly on eastbound 17th Street. Though the overpass over I-980 was not paved, that section was restriped and includes flex posts in the buffer, creating a separated bike lane. At the corner of 17th St and Brush St, a ride-up bulb-out was installed.

This curb-protected refuge makes waiting for the green light much more comfortable, since many drivers on 17th St make a right turn toward the freeway, and other drivers fly down Brush St coming off the freeway. The new bike lanes connect to existing buffered and separated lanes that reach Telegraph Ave, together making a 0.6-mile-long bikeway. Stay tuned for an even longer continuous route with the 18th St Complete Streets Paving Project between Mandela Pkwy and Market St, scheduled for construction later this year.

Upgraded

Bike Locker Upgrades

The Bicycle and Pedestrian Program upgraded all 12 electronic bike locker spaces at Frank Ogawa Plaza with new lockers that include in-locker cameras, reinforced double-wall doors, improved locks, and alert sensors — plus two extra-large spaces for e-bikes and cargo bikes. Four of the replaced locker spaces were in good enough shape to be relocated to Broadway at 20th Street, increasing the number of e-locker spaces at that location from eight to twelve. Riders can access and pay for lockers via the BikeLink app or on-site. Learn more at oaklandca.gov/Bicycle-eLockers.

Ongoing program

The Bike Fix at the Oakland Public Library

The Bike Fix keeps rolling at 81st Ave & Martin Luther King Jr. Branch Libraries. So far this year, the program has hosted 41 clinics, repaired 350 bikes, given away an additional 105 bikes, and served 805 neighbors. A June 6 Learn-to-Ride Rodeo with Bike East Bay and Bay Area Bike Project put 32 more bikes into young riders' hands.

With support from OakDOT's Bicycle and Pedestrian Program and new funding from Walk Oakland Bike Oakland (WOBO), the program expanded mechanic staffing and launched a Bike Mechanic Apprentice Program. Also new: four e-bike-hauled Book Libraries, a renovated repair shop at the 81st Ave Branch Library, and rides for Bike to Wherever Day and Bike!Bike! West 2026.

New this term

New BPAC Commissioners

The City of Oakland Bicyclist and Pedestrian Advisory Commission welcomed four new commissioners in 2026. Commissioners serve three-year terms; applications are accepted on a rolling basis and are due September 10, 2026 for the term beginning January 1, 2027. Details at oaklandca.gov/bpac.

Razzu Engen

Bikes to get around town whenever possible. He advocates for safe walking, biking, and public transportation — reducing car dependency to rein in local pollution and mitigate climate impacts.

Tonya Love

A mission-driven public health and policy professional with 15+ years advancing equity through government, nonprofit, and grassroots work. As former Chief of Staff for Councilmember Carroll Fife, she focused on bicyclist and pedestrian safety.

Jesse Olsen

An Oakland native and District 5 resident who bikes the greater East Bay most days. Helps run Biketopia, a nonprofit bike co-op, and is a regular ride leader and bike DJ for East Bay Bike Party.

Denise Schmidt

An avid e-biker and retired librarian, formerly of the San Francisco Public Library for 25 years. First arrived in the Bay Area in 1982 for UC Berkeley; moved to Oakland's District 4 in 1999.
